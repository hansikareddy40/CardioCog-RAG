\chapter{Conclusion and Scope for Further Research}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis set out to predict cognitive decline over the next three years for people without dementia at a clinic visit, to establish which kinds of information contribute to that prediction, and to explain each estimate in a form a clinician can check. Using the NACC database under a person-level split with nine held-out centres, the answers to the seven research questions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Progression to dementia within three years can be predicted with an AUROC of 0.94 in the combined cohort and 0.95 at held-out centres; within MCI the AUROC is about 0.84, and within cognitively normal participants about 0.88 with wide uncertainty. Cognitive decline measured as a one-point rise in CDR-SB is predicted with an AUROC of 0.86 (0.89 at held-out centres), 0.75 within MCI and 0.76 to 0.83 within cognitively normal participants, and the predicted change in CDR-SB is informative mainly for people who are already impaired.
\item Cardiovascular and medical history adds about 0.001 AUROC for dementia and nothing measurable for CDR-SB decline once current cognition is known.
\item Amyloid and tau PET measures did not produce a demonstrable gain over the clinical model with the follow-up currently available.
\item Neither deep tabular models nor the modality-encoder fusion network performed better than logistic regression or gradient-boosted trees.
\item Earlier visits added between 0.000 and 0.005 AUROC beyond the current visit.
\item A model trained with modality dropout remained close to specialist models when whole categories of information were missing, whereas a standard model became badly calibrated.
\item Each estimate can be explained with Shapley values, checked for implausible inputs, and summarised in a plain-language report with quoted published evidence.
\end{enumerate}

Most of the predictive information lies in the current cognitive and clinical state. The results are robust to the exclusion of participants with unknown outcomes, to reversed diagnoses and to retraining with different seeds, and the pipeline passed seventeen independent verification checks after two sources of information leakage had been found and corrected. Alongside the models, the work produced a PET image pipeline whose amyloid measurements agree with the PET core's (correlation 0.98), and a research prototype that checks the plausibility of its inputs and supports its output with a plain-language report and an evidence assistant restricted to published sources.

\section{Scope for Further Research}

\subsection{External and Temporal Validation}
The model should be evaluated on an independent cohort with different recruitment and on participants enrolled after the training period.

\subsection{Cognitively Normal Participants and Longer Horizons}
A model built specifically for cognitively normal participants, with a five- or ten-year horizon, would have more events and more room for cardiovascular and PET information to contribute.

\subsection{Richer Cardiovascular Exposure}
Repeated blood-pressure measurements over many visits, treatment history and imaging markers of small vessel disease could capture vascular burden better than yes/no history.

\subsection{PET with Longer Follow-up}
As more scanned participants reach three or more years of follow-up, the added value of amyloid and tau PET can be tested with adequate power, and MRI-based processing of the images can improve quantification.

\subsection{Evidence Assistant}
A larger, curated library, an independently written evaluation set and a stronger local language model would make the assistant more useful.

\section{Final Remarks}
The main finding of this work is a negative one, but it is supported by a controlled design and independent checks. In this cohort, the information that best predicts dementia within three years is the person's current cognitive and functional state. Long-term factors such as vascular history and genetics appear to be reflected in that state and add little independent information to a short-term prediction.
