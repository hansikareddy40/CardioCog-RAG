\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Many older adults who do not have dementia today will develop it within a few years, yet the information that clinics routinely collect about them is rarely combined into a single, explainable estimate of that risk. This thesis presents CardioCog, a framework that predicts cognitive decline over the next three years from demographic, cardiovascular, genetic and clinical information, adds amyloid and tau PET when it is available, explains each prediction, and supports it with published evidence.

The work uses the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center database (57,038 participants, 217,598 visits). Neuropsychological tests from different form versions were placed on one scale with demographically adjusted $z$-scores, and 48 features in five categories were built for every participant. Cognitive decline was defined in two ways: progression to a diagnosis of dementia (19,470 participants, 2,818 events) and a worsening of at least one point in the CDR Sum of Boxes (20,355 participants, 5,035 events), together with the predicted three-year change in that score. Participants were split by person, and nine whole centres were held out for evaluation.

Five classifiers, from logistic regression to an FT-Transformer, and a modality-encoder fusion network with attention all performed alike: AUROC 0.93 to 0.94 for dementia (0.94 to 0.95 at held-out centres) and about 0.86 for CDR-SB decline (0.89). Within participants with mild cognitive impairment the AUROC was 0.84 for dementia and 0.75 for decline. Most of the predictive signal came from current cognitive status. Cardiovascular and medical history added about 0.001 AUROC for dementia and nothing measurable for decline once cognition was known; earlier visits added at most 0.005, and PET measures produced no demonstrable gain. Training with modality dropout kept predictions calibrated when whole categories were missing. A separate PET image pipeline reproduced the PET core's amyloid measurements (correlation 0.98 over 318 scans). The models were built into a research prototype that checks the plausibility of its inputs and writes a plain-language report with quoted evidence, and the pipeline passed seventeen independent verification checks, which also found and corrected two sources of information leakage.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} dementia prediction, mild cognitive impairment, NACC, cardiovascular risk, explainable machine learning, SHAP, amyloid PET, retrieval-augmented generation.
